The Old Wanderer smiled through the chaos that just unfolded. It really took the others that long to recognize the howlers. Maybe they were overestimating their fellowship after all. Alex took a step back, to fully assess the situation. Three Howlers in the front, their long dark shaggy fur was surprisingly clean. Head to paw, they stood roughly half an adult tall - impressive for their kind. Teeth fletching. From the left, the right and the back, there were each one more. They were considerably smaller. It was no surprise that amongst everybody here Tex seemed to be least equipped to deal with that attack. The priest was holding the fork he was eating with just a moment ago and took a step away from the howlers in the front. Both Niff and Naff understood that the best way to deal with a howler was to scare it. With drawn swords, standing tall they approached the tall ones in lockstep and made noise - A gamble with good odds. If it were only those three the twins might have saved their leader without a fight - or not - it really was a solid matter of luck. The really smart one however was Clayat. She had grabbed one of the burning sticks out of the fire and made the howlers one after another run off starting with the smallest, saving the large three for the end.

Together with the twins protecting her back this was basically textbook behavior to fend off a brood of howlers. The one really concerning Alex was Wayaar: Just like the Old Wanderer themself Wyaar was just standing there, maybe fully trusting Clayat. However, opposed to Alex, they were not watching the group, they only had eyes for the Old Wanderer.

Alex hid their smile. “Is everyone recharged? I think we should not stick around for much longer. The tracks are getting weaker and I do not want to lose the civilian ...”

 

Tex nodded: “This was way too close, we should not expose ourselves like that again.” Breath. It was not lost on him that the civilians were dealing with that  situation better than he was. He felt like a failure, like his actions were putting the church to shame. How were they supposed to be the face of order in the realm, when civilians could not rely on them to deal with simple threats for them. Of course you would have decentralized arbiters of  Order when the priests would be weak like him.

Maybe this whole journey also was just a lesson to him of how much stronger he would need to be as a priest.

He needed to learn from the Civilians.

 

“Clayat?” He asked later when they were once again deeper in the woods. “Yes, sir?” “It seemed like you were quite skilled when dealing with those beasts, I want to thank you for that.” “Yeah, back in our home town we also had to deal with those animals,... Of course they were much smaller there, but fire scares them nonetheless.” Tex nodded. That makes sense, fire is inherently chaotic. There are branches of the church that reject open fires in general - “Puritans” is how the executioners refer to them. He always used to think that every priest should strive to be a puritan. However this instance threw a shadow of doubt over his mind. Clearly the pragmatists used fire for a reason. It is a tool after all and just like any tool it can bring both chaos and order to the realm. Some tools are just too efficient to be disregarded out of idealism.

 

The terrain got steeper and once again more rocky. The Womb is not particular known for its mountains but apparently some do stand there as well. Luckily they did not have to start climbing. Just after a couple of steps on a bigger rock the Old wanderer stopped. In front of them, there was a cave, mouth opening itself up smiling widely. A deep maw probably leading below stream level. 

“Did the beast go in there?” Tex asked nervously. The smile of the cave was evil. “I think so.” The tone of the mercenary was dry as he spoke. Was even he fearful or was Tex just imagining things to justify his own weakness? 

“Everyone prepare your weapons, we might fight a monster soon.”
