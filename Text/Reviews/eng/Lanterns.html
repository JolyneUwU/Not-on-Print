Lanterns 

 

So this will be a weird review. Initially I was intrigued by this show. The Green Lantern Corps were a part of the DC Canon, that I was not familiar with at all, but have heard good things about. And the creators were previously involved in projects that proved their credibility as nuanced showrunners (e.g. True Detective and Watchmen). Then I was even more interested when critics talked about how this show surprised them. It was supposed to be really grounded and a little subversive. And when the numbers came in to announce this particular show as the first real success of the new DC-Universe I just started watching it.

This was where the Review was doomed to get weird though: A miss-click made me unknowingly start the second episode instead of the first one. Did I quickly realize that? No! I was surprised about  how clever the show introduced its characters. Not a single word was spoken before I quickly understood the dynamic between Hal Jordan and John Stewart. As a Hangover-Type mystery we slowly unraveled the events from last night that put our Lanterns in the situation that they ended up in. As an untouchable threat on his closed off ranch, there was a character obviously established as a villain in disguise. And at the end, we got a small glimpse at the true size of the Lanterns universe. What a great pilot!

Except - as I said - this was not the pilot. This was episode two. I found that out as I wanted to start the next episode. Some shows could have been ruined for me through such a miss-click, but for this show I was only more intrigued. I now knew everything I felt I needed to know. Between writing and editing someone within the crew must also have realized that. So what was the actual pilot telling me, that justified its existence? A lot actually. I still think you could watch these episodes in either order, but there were necessary scenes justifying the existence of episode one beyond just overexplaining its plot to a stupid audience. We got a deeper dive into possible themes, a wider timeline indicating that the plot at least spans a decade, a new mystery and a couple other scenes that just were too good to be potentially cut. Aside from that I have to say that the introduction that we now got for John Stewart might be less subtle but instead of subtlety we opened a can into a more thematically complex characterisation that will pay off in later episodes. As a first experience I preferred starting with episode two. As a complete show it was a necessity to prepare the audience for the unique upbringing John Stewart had.

From that moment I continued to watch the show in chronological order. Was I contemplating keeping that accidental experiment going? Yes, this could have made for an interesting text to find out how a show is watched in non-chronological order. Ultimately I decided to just be happy that this accident did not already spoil the show for me, because it would have been a shame to ruin a great show through a bold experiment. 

Now how was the show aside from that experiment? Great! I like the thematic questions this show asked about the nature of both fear and races. As I understood it, they took themes from the general myth and reworked them in a deconstructive nuanced way. As an outsider it just felt intuitive and organic to me. The characters and actors are also a stand out quality of this show and I like the gravity that comes with the few action scenes that the show offers. Much like those, the superpowers are also used in a quality over quantity way. So if you want a grounded, character-driven show, you will get quality here.

You won’t get the epic space opera that the Green Lanterns are usually known for, but what you get instead is adding a lot of weight to characters and mythos of the DC Universe. 

In some way one might compare this show to Andor from the Star Wars Universe. I would say: Don’t expect a show as great as Andor, but expect a great show with comparable ambitions like Andor and you won’t be disappointed.

 

Short: A great show where you could switch the first two episodes for some more mystery fun.
